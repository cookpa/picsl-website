title: Medial temporal lobe asymmetry in temporal lobe epilepsy at 7T

date: '2019-06-01'
author: sandhitsu-sandy-das
---

Mesial temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) is a common neurological disorder affecting the hippocampus and surrounding medial temporal lobe (MTL). Whole-brain network changes in TLE had been studied, but not the functional network of the MTL at the level of its subregions.

In *Human Brain Mapping*, Preya Shah, [Laura Wisse](/members/laura-wisse.html), [Paul Yushkevich](/members/paul-yushkevich.html), [Sandhitsu Das](/members/sandhitsu-sandy-das.html), Kathryn Davis and colleagues used high-resolution 7T T2-weighted MRI and resting-state fMRI in patients with unilateral, drug-resistant TLE and in healthy controls. Patients were split into those with mesial temporal sclerosis (TLE-MTS) and those whose MRI showed no lesion (TLE-NL). An automated multi-atlas pipeline delineated 10 MTL subregions per hemisphere.

- In volume, TLE-MTS patients had smaller hippocampal subfields on the side of seizure onset, while TLE-NL patients showed only a mild decrease in whole-hippocampal volume.
- In function, the CA1 subfield and whole hippocampus showed opposite asymmetries: increased connectivity on the seizure side in TLE-NL, and decreased connectivity in TLE-MTS.

Network properties within the MTL can therefore distinguish TLE subtypes, and high-resolution MRI could improve localization for patients being considered for surgery.
