title: Deep learning measures of tau and TDP-43 explain medial temporal lobe atrophy
layout: post
date: '2024-09-03'
author: paul-yushkevich
---

The medial temporal lobe (MTL) is a hotspot for neuropathology, and MTL atrophy is widely used as a biomarker of cognitive decline. Because several proteins accumulate there at once, it has been hard to say which pathology drives the atrophy.

In a study in *Acta Neuropathologica*, [Amanda Denning](/members/amanda-denning.html), [Paul Yushkevich](/members/paul-yushkevich.html) and colleagues developed two deep learning algorithms that measure phosphorylated tau (p-tau) and TDP-43 pathology on digitized histology sections with annotated MTL subregions. The focus was on Alzheimer's disease and limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy (LATE).

Key findings:

- Both quantitative measures agree closely with expert visual ratings and distinguish well between pathology stages.
- In 140 cases with MRI scans taken during life, p-tau pathology was associated with structure across MTL subregions, while TDP-43 had more limited associations with the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex.
- Quantitative p-tau measures explained antemortem cortical thickness and volume significantly better than semi-quantitative ratings, and still showed associations in severe AD cases where the ratings hit a ceiling.

The results argue for quantitative neuropathology in future studies of how pathology relates to brain structure.
